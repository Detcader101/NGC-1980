CHANGE 01

The images caught what nonblank checks missed

The parachute camera blended its aim down toward the ground as altitude increased, cropping the canopy. It now keeps body and canopy together while retaining a bounded horizontal landing-zone cue. The prone Marine bent a knee through the terrain and brought the boot back up, making the leg look detached; the knee pull now stays in the prone ground plane. The Osprey delivery fixture now snaps its inspection camera after simulation fast-forwarding.

What the evidence shows

The original screenshots show the cropped parachutes, disconnected-looking boot and an Osprey delivery view aimed at a hillside. Current images show the complete lead canopy, continuous prone legs, and the Osprey with its landed troops. The before/after labels identify those actual captured versions. These are visual corrections, not frame-rate improvement claims.

  • The canopy case projects actual visible mesh/line vertices through the game camera before and after sampling/capture. An intermediate test used a world-aligned box; its impossible corners wrongly failed the margin even though every visible vertex fit. That failed run is retained.
  • The production prone model passes a 72-phase mesh/joint clearance scan: the original knee reached 16.4 cm below flat ground; corrected leg/boot vertices stay above it.
  • Both transport delivery views require the aircraft and at least three landed troops in frame, with terrain line of sight.

Limits of this check

  • The scripted parachute release bypasses the approach and jumpmaster checklist. The camera test is desktop only.
  • The prone correction does not add terrain-following foot IK. Transport delivery framing uses a dev camera snap, while troop motion remains the production simulation.
