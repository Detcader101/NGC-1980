CHANGE 01

Capture the running world

The benchmark skill now records frame intervals and render counters, takes screenshots, and saves short MP4 clips of the full game page. Each run identifies its source, game content, scene, seed, browser, renderer and viewport. Recordings happen after measurement; decoding the saved clip produces a poster and a three-frame contact sheet.

What the evidence shows

The coastal view shows the Huey above the water, distant haze, small clusters of specks below the aircraft and the normal HUD. The decoded clip frames show changes in the sea and rotor. The two runs use unchanged game content, so differences in their timings show repeatability limits rather than a performance gain.

  • Both desktop runs keep the helicopter alive, retain naturally populated sea life and produce nonblank coastal images.
  • The saved clips decode at three different times; their actual encoded duration and requested wall window are both preserved.

Limits of this check

  • This is a seeded, peaceful hover fixture with an automatic collective adjustment. It does not establish manual flight endurance or busy-war performance.

OPEN ITEMS

Remaining work and limits

  • These short 30-interval samples verify the workflow. Use longer repeated samples on the intended GPU for performance decisions.
  • All included game runs use Chrome 156 and SwiftShader under WSL. Software rendering is slow and uneven; the reported values do not predict headset or consumer GPU performance.
  • Native MP4 capture is silent and can drop initial or intermediate frames. Encoded duration can be shorter than its requested wall-time window; encoded frame rate is never game FPS.
  • Three decoded frames help inspect a clip but cannot prove every transition is free of pop-in. This report makes no new scenery-LOD correctness claim.
  • The game content is unchanged in this tooling pass. Existing war-balance, direct-hand-interaction and hardware-qualification ambitions remain open.
  • Visual finding: the desktop VR-availability panel overlaps the lower-right research area. Functional assertions pass, but this overlap deserves a follow-up UI fix.
